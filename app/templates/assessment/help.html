🧠 What is a Behavioural Self-Assessment?

A behavioural self-assessment is a structured set of questions designed to help you reflect on aspects of your thinking, emotions, behaviour, or skills. Assessments like the ones on AssessMe ask you to rate or choose responses that best describe how you typically think, feel, or act.

These tools are widely used in educational, occupational, and coaching contexts to support self-awareness and personal development. They are not diagnostic tests and should not be treated as such.

⚙️ How AssessMe Works
  1. Choose an assessment — select the type that matches your goal.
  2. Provide context — enter basic information to help personalise your results.
  3. Answer questions — respond honestly; there are no right or wrong answers.
  4. Review your responses — check your answers before submitting.
  5. Receive a report — view your score and plain-language observations.
  6. Read guidance — access personalised reflection suggestions.
  7. Track over time — revisit your history to observe change.
📊 Interpreting Your Results

Results are presented as a percentage score and a descriptive band (High / Moderate / Low). These bands are general descriptors only.

High Responses lean toward the positive or confident end of the scale. This may reflect current strengths, positive mood, or strong self-awareness.
Moderate Mixed responses. This is the most common range and reflects a blend of areas of strength and areas for growth.
Low Responses lean toward the lower end. This may reflect a difficult period, areas needing attention, or simply how you felt on the day.
💡 Results can vary significantly based on mood, time of day, and how questions are interpreted. A single score does not define you.
⚠️ Limitations of Automated Assessments
⚠️ Understanding these limitations is essential for responsible use of this tool.
  • Not a clinical diagnosis: No automated assessment can diagnose a medical or psychological condition.
  • Self-report bias: Responses reflect how you see yourself, which may differ from how others perceive you.
  • Moment-in-time snapshot: Results reflect your state when you answered — they are not fixed or permanent.
  • Cultural and language context: Questions may carry different meanings across cultural backgrounds.
  • No substitute for professional advice: If you have concerns about your mental health, please consult a qualified professional.
  • Uncertainty in AI-assisted scoring: Scoring models are based on general patterns and may not accurately reflect your individual experience.
🔒 Privacy & Data Use
  • We collect only the information needed to generate and contextualise your results.
  • Your assessment responses and results are visible only to you.
  • We do not sell, share, or disclose your data to third parties.
  • You may delete your assessment records at any time from the History page.
  • Your account can be deleted by contacting support.
  • We use standard security practices to protect stored data.
🩺 Where to Find Professional Support

If you are experiencing difficulties with mental health, wellbeing, or personal development, the following types of professionals can help:

  • General Practitioner (GP / Doctor) — first point of contact for health concerns.
  • Psychologist or Therapist — trained in assessment and talking therapies.
  • Counsellor — support for emotional and personal difficulties.
  • Career advisor or coach — guidance for career and development questions.
  • School or university counselling services — free support for students.
📞 In a crisis? Contact your local emergency services (e.g. 999, 911) or a crisis helpline. In the UK: Samaritans 116 123 (free, 24/7). In the US: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988).
